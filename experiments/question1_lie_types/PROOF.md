# Question 1 for regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties of simply-laced type: a proof

Notation. `Φ` is a root system in a Euclidean space `V` with Weyl group `W`, `Δ ⊂ Φ⁺` the simple
and positive roots, `I ⊂ Φ⁺` a lower order ideal, `X = Hess(I) ⊂ G/B` the regular semisimple
Hessenberg variety. Its fixed points are the `w ∈ W`, the tangent weights at `w` are `−w(α)`,
`α ∈ I`, the invariant curves at `w` join `w` to `w s_α`, and `H^odd(X) = 0`
(De Mari–Procesi–Shayman). The weights at a fixed point are pairwise independent, so `X` is a GKM
manifold. `M(I)` is the linear matroid of `I ⊂ V`, `L(I)` its lattice of flats, and for a flat
`F`, `W_F ⊂ W` is the subgroup generated by the reflections `s_α`, `α ∈ F`.

## Proposition 1 (face poset in any type)

`S(X) ≅ {(F, wW_F) : F ∈ L(I), w ∈ W}`, ordered by `(F, wW_F) ⩽ (F', w'W_{F'})` iff `F ⊂ F'` and
`wW_F ⊂ w'W_{F'}`. The rank of `(F, wW_F)` is `rk F`, and the number of fixed points below it is
`ν = |W_F|`. In particular `S(X)_{⩾ w} ≅ L(I)` for every `w`.

*Proof.* By Ayzenberg–Cherepanov the faces through `w` correspond to the flats of the matroid of
weights at `w`, i.e. to the flats `F` of `M(I)`: the face `Y` is the component through `w` of
`X^H`, where `H ⊂ T` is the identity component of the common kernel of the characters `w(α)`,
`α ∈ F`. `Y` is a smooth projective `T`-variety with isolated fixed points, so any two of its fixed
points are joined by a chain of `T`-invariant curves of `Y`, and an invariant curve of `X` at a
fixed point `v ∈ Y` lies in `Y` iff its weight vanishes on `H`, i.e. lies in the span of `w(F)`.
Write `v = wu`. The curves at `v` have weights `wu(β)`, `β ∈ I`, and lead to `wus_β`. Starting from
`u = 1` the allowed `β` are those of `I ∩ span F = F`, so `u` stays in `W_F`; and for `u ∈ W_F`,
which preserves `span F`, the condition `u(β) ∈ span F` is again `β ∈ F`. Hence `Y^T = wW_F`. The
tangent weights of `Y` at `w` recover `F`, and a face is determined by its tangent space at one of
its points, so different pairs give different faces. Inclusion of faces is inclusion of their
tangent weights at a common fixed point. ∎

For type `A` and `I` the ideal of a Hessenberg function `h`, flats are clusterings of `Γ_h`, `W_F`
is the product of the symmetric groups of the blocks, and this is the cluster-permutohedron.

## Proposition 2 (what the Betti numbers depend on)

Let `I ⊂ Φ⁺`, `I' ⊂ Φ'⁺` be ideals. Suppose there is a linear isomorphism `g : V → V'` with
`g W g⁻¹ = W'` that maps the set of lines `{Rα : α ∈ I}` onto `{Rα' : α' ∈ I'}`. Then
`H*_T(Hess(I)) ≅ H*_{T'}(Hess(I'))` as graded rings, compatibly with the isomorphism
`H*(BT) ≅ H*(BT')` induced by `g`, and the Betti numbers are equal.

*Proof.* The GKM graph of `Hess(I)` has vertices `W`, edges `{w, ws_α}` for `α ∈ I`, and the edge
at `w` is labelled `±w(α)`; it depends only on `W` and the lines of `I`. The map `w ↦ gwg⁻¹`
sends the edge `{w, ws_α}` to `{gwg⁻¹, gwg⁻¹ s_{gα}}`, an edge of the second graph, and its label
`w(α)` to `g(w(α)) = (gwg⁻¹)(gα)`, a multiple of the label of that edge. By the GKM theorem
(rational coefficients) the equivariant cohomology rings correspond, and since both varieties are
equivariantly formal their Poincaré polynomials are the Hilbert series of `H*_T` times
`(1 − q)^{rank}`. ∎

Diagram automorphisms and the duality `α ↔ α^∨` are of this form, which explains the coincidences
found in `coincidences.log`.

## Lemma 3 (induced cycles)

Let `Φ` be simply laced, roots normalised to `(α, α) = 2`, and let `α_1, …, α_k` (`k ⩾ 3`) be roots
such that `(α_i, α_{i+1}) = ±1` (indices mod `k`) and all other pairs are orthogonal. Let
`ε = ∏_i (α_i, α_{i+1})`. Then `dim span(α_1, …, α_k) = k − 1` if `ε = (−1)^k`, and `k` otherwise.

*Proof.* The Gram matrix is `2·Id + A`, with `A` the signed adjacency matrix of a `k`-cycle.
Changing the signs of some `α_i` conjugates `A` by a diagonal sign matrix and preserves `ε`, so we
may assume all entries of `A` are `−1` except possibly one, which is `−1` iff `ε = (−1)^k`. In that
case `2·Id + A` is the Cartan matrix of the affine diagram of type `A_{k−1}`, of corank 1.
Otherwise `−A` is the adjacency matrix of a cycle with one negative edge, whose eigenvalues are
`2cos((2j+1)π/k)`, `j = 0, …, k−1`; none of them is `2`, so `2·Id + A` is nonsingular. ∎

(Checked by computer for all induced cycles of length at most 8 among the positive roots of
`A_2–A_6`, `D_4–D_6`, and for `E_6`, `E_7` up to length 7 and 6: `simply_laced_check.py`.)

## Theorem 4 (rigidity, simply-laced types)

Let `Φ`, `Φ'` be simply-laced root systems (types `A`, `D`, `E` and their products) and `I`, `I'`
lower ideals containing all simple roots. Suppose there is an isomorphism of lattices
`L(I) → L(I')` that preserves `ν` on the elements of rank 2. Then there is an isometry
`g : V → V'` with `gWg⁻¹ = W'` mapping the lines of `I` onto the lines of `I'`.

*Proof.* The isomorphism restricts to a bijection `φ : I → I'` of atoms. For distinct `α, β ∈ I` the
plane they span contains no further root if `(α, β) = 0`, and is a plane of type `A_2` otherwise;
so the join `α ∨ β` has `ν = 4` in the first case and `ν = 6` in the second (two reflections of an
`A_2`-plane already generate its group). Hence `φ` preserves orthogonality: it is an isomorphism of
the non-orthogonality graphs `N(I) → N(I')`.

Put the sign of `(α, β)` on each edge of `N(I)`. For an induced cycle `C` of `N(I)`, the rank of
the join of its atoms in `L(I)` is `dim span C`, which by Lemma 3 determines the product of the
signs around `C`. As `φ` preserves induced cycles and ranks of joins, the two signed graphs have
the same sign product on every induced cycle. The induced cycles span the cycle space of a graph,
and the sign product is a homomorphism from the cycle space to `{±1}`; so the products agree on all
cycles, and two signings of a graph with this property differ by switching (Harary; Zaslavsky).
That is, there are signs `d_α = ±1` with `(φα, φβ) = d_α d_β (α, β)` for all `α, β ∈ I`.

The vectors `α ∈ I` and `d_α φ(α) ∈ ±I'` therefore have the same Gram matrix. `I` contains the
simple roots, so it spans `V`, and likewise `I'` spans `V'`; hence there is a unique isometry `g`
with `g(α) = d_α φ(α)`. It maps the lines of `I` to the lines of `I'`, and
`g s_α g⁻¹ = s_{φ(α)}`. Since `W` is generated by the simple reflections, which are among the
`s_α`, `α ∈ I`, and all `s_α` lie in `W`, we get `gWg⁻¹ = ⟨s_{α'} : α' ∈ I'⟩ = W'`. ∎

## Corollary 5 (Question 1 in types A, D, E)

Let `X = Hess(I)` and `X' = Hess(I')` be connected regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties of
simply-laced types. If `S(X) ≅ S(X')`, then `β_j(X) = β_j(X')` for all `j`, and `H*_T(X) ≅ H*_T(X')`
as graded rings, compatibly with an isomorphism of the tori.

*Proof.* An isomorphism of face posets maps a minimal element to a minimal element and restricts
to an isomorphism of the upper intervals, which are `L(I)` and `L(I')` by Proposition 1, preserving
the number `ν` of minimal elements below each element. Apply Theorem 4 and Proposition 2. ∎

Remarks.

1. For type `A` this is a second proof of Corollary 5.6 of the paper for Hessenberg varieties, not
   using twins: the graph `Γ_h` is recovered as the configuration of lines `R(e_i − e_j)`.
2. *Disconnected varieties.* If `I` does not contain all simple roots, let `J` be the set of simple
   roots in `I`. Then `I ⊂ Φ_J⁺`, `Hess(I)` is the disjoint union of `|W|/|W_J|` copies of the
   Hessenberg variety of `(Φ_J, I)`, and the proof gives an isometry on the span of `I` conjugating
   `W_J = ⟨s_α : α ∈ I⟩`. The number of copies is the number of minimal elements divided by `|W_J|`.
   So the corollary holds for all ideals of simply-laced types.
3. *Other types.* Proposition 1 and Proposition 2 hold in every type. What fails is the first step
   of Theorem 4: in a plane of type `B_2` or `G_2` containing three or more lines of `I`, `ν` is the
   order of the whole dihedral group and no longer tells which pairs of lines are orthogonal. The
   sweep (`RESULTS.md`) shows that for all ideals of `B_2–B_5, C_3–C_5, F_4, G_2` the conclusion
   still holds. Proving it needs an argument that recovers the angles inside such planes from the
   rest of the lattice.

# Track D outside type A: results

Scripts: `lie_types.py` (log `lie_types.log`) and `coincidences.py` (log `coincidences.log`).
Run: `../../engine/.venv/bin/python lie_types.py A1 A2 A3 A4 A5 B2 G2 B3 C3 D4 B4 C4 F4 D5 B5 C5`.

## Question

Regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties exist in every Lie type: for a root system `Φ` with Weyl
group `W` and a lower order ideal `I` of the poset of positive roots, `Hess(I) ⊂ G/B` is smooth of
dimension `|I|`, has fixed points `w ∈ W`, tangent weights `w(−I)` at `w`, even cohomology, and
(De Mari–Procesi–Shayman)

    P_I(q) = Σ_{w ∈ W} q^{#{α ∈ I : w(α) < 0}}.

It is connected iff `I` contains all simple roots. **Does the face poset determine `P_I`?** In type
A this is Theorem C, proved by reconstructing the graph; there the local matroids are graphic.
Outside type A they are not, and the star argument does not apply.

## The face poset in any type

The `T`-invariant curves at `w` join `w` to `w s_α`, `α ∈ I`. A face through `w` is a component of
the fixed set of a subtorus; its tangent weights at `w` are `w(F)` for a flat `F` of the linear
matroid `M(I)`, and its fixed points form the coset `w W_F`, where `W_F` is generated by the
reflections `s_α`, `α ∈ F`. Hence

    S(Hess(I)) = { (F, w W_F) : F a flat of M(I), w ∈ W },  ordered by inclusion,

of rank `rk F`, with `ν(F, wW_F) = |W_F|` fixed points below. For type A this is the
cluster-permutohedron (blocks ↔ `F`, assignments ↔ cosets). `W` acts on the poset by left
multiplication, transitively on minimal elements, so above every minimal element one sees the
same **`ν`-labelled lattice of flats** `(L(I), ν)`. The face poset determines `|W|` (the number of
minimal elements) and `(L(I), ν)`.

The poset depends only on `W` and the lines `Rα`, `α ∈ I`, and `P_I` only on `W` and the rays
`R_{>0}α`. So two kinds of coincidence are automatic and harmless: a **diagram automorphism**
(the varieties are isomorphic) and **duality** `α ↔ α^∨` (types `B_n`/`C_n`, and `B₂`, `G₂`, `F₄`
with themselves: same lines, same `W`, same signs, so the same poset and the same `P`).

## Result 1: no counterexample

For every ideal of the root systems `A₁–A₅, B₂–B₅, C₃–C₅, D₄, D₅, F₄, G₂` — 1,230 ideals, 577 of
them connected — the pair `(|W|, (L(I), ν))`, compared up to isomorphism with nauty and across
types, determines the Poincaré polynomial:

| | Ideals | Classes of `(|W|, L, ν)` | Poincaré polynomials | Classes with two different `P` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| connected (all simple roots) | 577 | 460 | 396 | **0** |
| all | 1,230 | 749 | 661 | **0** |

So **Question 1 has the answer yes for all regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties of these 16
types**, and already a small part of the face poset (one upper interval with `ν`) suffices.

## Result 2: the poset is rigid, as in Theorem A

`coincidences.py` asks why two ideals share `(|W|, L, ν)`. For connected ideals, all 122 pairs are
explained:

| Explanation | Pairs |
|---|---|
| diagram automorphism (same type) | 57 |
| duality (`B_n ↔ C_n`; `B₂`, `G₂`, `F₄` self-dual) | 65 |
| only an element of `W` mapping lines to lines | 0 |
| none | 0 |

In other words, in these types **the `ν`-labelled lattice determines the pair (Weyl group, lines
of `I`) up to a diagram automorphism and duality** — the analogue of "`Cl_Γ` determines `Γ`". Since
both operations preserve `P_I` for obvious reasons, this gives a proof-shaped reason for Result 1
and a precise statement to prove:

> **Conjecture.** Let `I ⊂ Φ⁺` and `I' ⊂ Φ'⁺` be lower ideals containing all simple roots. If
> `S(Hess(I)) ≅ S(Hess(I'))`, then there is a linear isomorphism of the ambient spaces that
> conjugates `W` to `W'`, maps the positive rays of `Φ` to those of `Φ'` or of `Φ'^∨`, and maps the
> lines of `I` to the lines of `I'`. In particular the Betti numbers agree.

Verified here for rank at most 4, `A₅`, `D₅`, `B₅` and `C₅`. A proof would start as in type A: for
two atoms `α, β` above a minimal element, `ν(α ∨ β)` is the order of the reflection group
generated by the roots of `I` in the plane of `α, β` (4, 6, 8 or 12), which records the angles
between the lines; what replaces the star argument is the reconstruction of a root subsystem from
these angles.

For disconnected ideals the same sweep shows equal `P` in every class, with coincidences of two
more kinds (371 pairs related by an element of `W`, 160 by neither). These come from the same
smaller variety sitting in different Levi subgroups: `Hess(I)` is then a disjoint union of
`|W|/|W_J|` copies of a Hessenberg variety of the Levi subsystem spanned by the simple roots in
`I`, and only that subsystem with its ideal matters.

## Checks

- Type A reproduces the known data: 5, 14, 42, 132 ideals; face counts of `Cl(K₃)` are
  `6, 9, 1`; all coincidences among connected type-A ideals are the reversal of the Hessenberg
  function.
- Ideal counts match the Catalan numbers of the types (`B₃`: 20, `D₄`: 50, `B₄`: 70, `F₄`: 105,
  `G₂`: 8, `D₅`: 182, `B₅`: 252).

## Limits

- `E₆, E₇, E₈`, and ranks above 5, are not covered (`E₆` has 833 ideals and `|W| = 51,840`; feasible
  with a compiled inner loop, not with this script).
- The face-poset description above is derived from the GKM graph and connectedness of fixed
  components; in type A it agrees with Ayzenberg–Buchstaber's theorem, in other types it is not
  taken from the literature.
- The comparison uses `(|W|, L, ν)`, which the face poset determines. It is a sufficient test for a
  "yes"; had two ideals with equal `(|W|, L, ν)` had different `P`, the full posets would have had
  to be compared.
- Partial flag varieties `G/P` and partial Hessenberg varieties outside type A are not included.

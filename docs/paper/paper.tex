\title{Face posets, local weight matroids and Betti numbers of torus actions}
\author{David A.~Mikheev}
\date{Draft, \today}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Let a compact torus act on a closed manifold with isolated fixed points. Its face poset $S(X)$ is covered by the lattices of flats of the linear matroids of tangent weights at the fixed points. We study how much of the cohomology these combinatorial data see. First, the local equivariant cohomology $\HT(X,X\setminus pt)$, embedded in $\HT(pt)$ by restriction, is the principal ideal generated by the product of the tangent weights; it therefore determines the weights up to scalars, with multiplicities, and hence the local lattice $S(X)_{\geqslant pt}$. Second, the local weight matroids do not determine the equivariant cohomology: two connected regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties in $\Fl(\C^5)$, with actions of complexity one, have isomorphic weight matroids at all fixed points but different Betti numbers. Third, the global face poset does determine the Betti numbers for every regular semisimple Hessenberg variety of type~A, full or partial, and for the isospectral manifolds of Ayzenberg and Buchstaber. The reason is rigidity: the cluster-permutohedron $\Cl_\Gamma$ determines the graph $\Gamma$ up to isomorphism, by an elementary argument that recovers the vertices of $\Gamma$ from stars of edges. Fourth, the same holds for regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties of all simply-laced Lie types: there the face poset determines the Weyl group together with the lines spanned by the roots of the Hessenberg ideal, up to isometry. Fifth, we give a formula for the Poincaré polynomial as a sum over the face poset, $P_X(q)=\sum_\sigma(q-1)^{\operatorname{rk}\sigma}e_\sigma(q)$, in which $e_\sigma$ is a sum of Tutte polynomials of the local matroids of negative weights; it reduces to the $h$-vector formula for toric manifolds.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================

Throughout, a \emph{torus action} is an effective action of a compact torus $T=T^k$ on a closed smooth manifold $X=X^{2n}$ with a finite nonempty set of fixed points. Toric topology studies such actions of complexity $n-k$ zero by combinatorial means~\cite{BP15}. Buchstaber and Terzić extended these methods to positive complexity~\cite{BT19a,BT19b}, and Ayzenberg, Masuda and their coauthors made the \emph{face poset} $S(X)$ of the action the central combinatorial object~\cite{AC22,AMS23,AB24}. Its elements are the closures of the components of the orbit type strata of $X/T$ that contain a fixed point, ordered by inclusion. By a theorem of Ayzenberg and Cherepanov~\cite{AC22}, $S(X)$ is \emph{locally geometric}: for a fixed point $p$, the faces containing $p$ form the lattice of flats of the linear matroid of the tangent weights at~$p$.

This paper asks what these combinatorial data know about the cohomology of $X$. We prove five results: one local, three on the face poset, and a formula.

\begin{theoremA}[Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let $pt$ be a fixed point with tangent weights $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\in H^2(BT;\Q)$. Restriction identifies $\HT(X,X\setminus pt)$ with the principal ideal $(\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_n)$ of $\HT(pt)=H^*(BT;\Q)$. This ideal determines the multiset of weights up to nonzero scalars, hence the linear matroid of weights at $pt$ with its parallel classes, and the lattice $S(X)_{\geqslant pt}$.
\end{theoremA}

The point of Theorem~A is that no independence assumption on the weights is needed. The familiar GKM argument computes the ideal as an intersection $\bigcap_j(\alpha_j)$, which equals the product only when the weights are pairwise independent and loses multiplicities otherwise. We use the Thom isomorphism and unique factorization instead.

The local data are far from determining the global cohomology. The Chow ring~\cite{AHK18} and the Orlik--Solomon algebra~\cite{OT92} of a matroid depend only on the matroid, and so do the augmented Chow ring, the intersection cohomology module~\cite{BHMPW20,BHMPW22} and the Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomial~\cite{EPW16}. The following therefore shows that none of them, taken at all fixed points, determines $\HT(X)$.

\begin{theoremB}[Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample}]
Let $M=\diag(1,2,3,4,5)$. The regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties $X_1=\Hess(M,(2,3,5,5,5))$ and $X_2=\Hess(M,(2,4,4,5,5))$ in $\Fl(\C^5)$ are connected GKM manifolds with the same $120$ fixed points and torus actions of complexity one. At every fixed point of either variety the weight matroid is $U_{2,3}\oplus U_{1,1}\oplus U_{1,1}$. Their Poincaré polynomials are $1+17q+42q^2+42q^3+17q^4+q^5$ and $1+12q+47q^2+47q^3+12q^4+q^5$, where $q$ has degree~$2$.
\end{theoremB}

The global face posets of $X_1$ and $X_2$ are different. This leaves the question, raised in~\cite{Mik22}, whether the face poset determines the Betti numbers of an equivariantly formal action. In complexity zero it does: for a quasitoric manifold the Betti numbers are the $h$-numbers of the orbit polytope~\cite{BP15}. Our third result answers the question for the main family of examples in positive complexity.

\begin{theoremC}[Theorems~\ref{thm:cl-rigid} and~\ref{thm:q1-partial}, Corollary~\ref{cor:q1-hess}]
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\arabic*)]
\item For simple graphs $\Gamma,\Gamma'$, an isomorphism of cluster-permutohedra $\Cl_\Gamma\cong\Cl_{\Gamma'}$ implies $\Gamma\cong\Gamma'$.
\item Let $X$ and $X'$ each be an isospectral manifold $M_{\Gamma,\lambda}$ or a regular semisimple Hessenberg variety $\Hess(M,h)$ of type~A, with $\Gamma$, resp.\ $\Gamma_h$, connected. If $S(X)\cong S(X')$, then $X$ and $X'$ have the same Betti numbers and isomorphic equivariant cohomology rings.
\item The same conclusion about Betti numbers holds for partial regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties, including all partial flag varieties.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theoremC}

The cluster-permutohedron $\Cl_\Gamma$ is the face poset of the isospectral manifold $M_{\Gamma,\lambda}$, and through the twin construction of Ayzenberg and Buchstaber also of $\Hess(M,h)$ when $\Gamma=\Gamma_h$~\cite{AB21,AB24}. Part~(1) is purely combinatorial. Counting the minimal elements below joins of pairs of atoms tells which edges of $\Gamma$ meet, i.e.\ recovers the line graph, and ranks of joins tell which triples of edges form triangles. These two data determine $\Gamma$ by an elementary star argument (Proposition~\ref{prop:edge-iso}). Unlike Whitney's line graph theorem~\cite{Whi32,Jun66}, it has no exceptions. For partial flags we combine the same argument with a fibration over the partial Hessenberg variety.

Theorem~C does not give a formula for the Betti numbers in terms of the poset. It shows that in these families the face poset remembers the whole graph, and the graph remembers the manifold. A counterexample to the general question, if one exists, must therefore come from outside type~A. Regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties exist in every Lie type, and our fourth result excludes half of the types.

\begin{theoremD}[Theorem~\ref{thm:simply-laced}, Corollary~\ref{cor:q1-ade}]
Let $X$ and $X'$ be regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties of simply-laced types, given by ideals $I\subset\Phi^+$ and $I'\subset\Phi'^+$. If $S(X)\cong S(X')$, then there is an isometry between the spans of $I$ and $I'$ that maps the lines spanned by the roots of $I$ onto those of $I'$ and conjugates the reflection groups they generate. Consequently $X$ and $X'$ have the same Betti numbers and isomorphic equivariant cohomology rings.
\end{theoremD}

The proof reads the angles between roots from the numbers of fixed points on the faces of rank~$2$, and the signs of the Gram matrix from the ranks of joins along induced cycles. For the types $B$, $C$, $F_4$ and $G_2$ the same conclusion holds for all ideals in rank at most~$5$ by computation (Section~\ref{sec:lie}), but the first step of the proof fails and we do not have an argument.

Finally, although the face poset alone does not give a formula for the Betti numbers, it does so together with the signs of the weights.

\begin{theoremE}[Theorem~\ref{thm:strata}]
Let $\xi$ be a generic one-parameter subgroup for which $P_X(q)=\sum_pq^{\operatorname{ind}(p)}$, and for a face $\sigma$ and a fixed point $p\in\sigma$ let $N_p(\sigma)$ be the set of weights of $\sigma$ at $p$ that are negative on~$\xi$. Then
\[
P_X(q)=\sum_{\sigma\in S(X)}(q-1)^{\operatorname{rk}\sigma}e_\sigma(q),\qquad e_\sigma(q)=\sum_pT_{M(N_p(\sigma))}(1,q),
\]
where the inner sum is over the fixed points $p$ of $\sigma$ at which $N_p(\sigma)$ spans the weights of $\sigma$, and $T_M$ is the Tutte polynomial. The polynomials $e_\sigma$ do not depend on~$\xi$, and $e_\sigma=1$ when the weights of $\sigma$ are linearly independent.
\end{theoremE}

For a toric manifold this is $h(q)=\sum_\sigma(q-1)^{\dim\sigma}$. In general $e_\sigma$ measures the failure of the action on $\sigma$ to be toric, and it explains Theorem~B: the two varieties there have the same $e_\sigma$ on their top faces and differ only in the numbers of lower faces.

\paragraph{Computations and formalization.}
All computations cited in this paper were done with independent programs, which are available with their output at \url{https://github.com/davidmiheev/matroid_toric}. The Betti numbers in Theorem~B were computed both from the Białynicki-Birula formula and, independently, from the GKM presentation of $\HT$ in Macaulay2~\cite{M2}. Part~(1) of Theorem~C was confirmed by computing exact poset isomorphism classes, with nauty's Traces~\cite{MP14}, for all $208$ graphs with at most $6$ vertices. Before the proof, Theorem~C(2) and~(3) were verified by direct computation for all full and partial Hessenberg varieties with $n\leqslant7$ (714 and 2\,940 cases). Part~(1) of Theorem~C (Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-rigid}, including Proposition~\ref{prop:edge-iso}) is formally verified in Lean~4 with Mathlib, in the same repository, with the cluster-permutohedron encoded as maps from vertices to label sets ordered pointwise. The lemma on induced cycles behind Theorem~D was checked for $A_2$--$A_6$, $D_4$--$D_6$, $E_6$ and $E_7$, and Theorem~E against a recursive computation of the $e_\sigma$ for all connected Hessenberg varieties with $n\leqslant5$. So is the algebraic step of Theorem~A, Lemma~\ref{lem:ufd}(2), together with the lattice of flats of a matroid, Whitney's Boolean expansion of its characteristic polynomial and Philip Hall's theorem.

\paragraph{Conventions.}
Cohomology has rational coefficients. We identify $H^*(BT)=\Sym(\mathfrak t^*_\Q)\cong\Q[t_1,\dots,t_k]$ with $\deg t_i=2$ and $H^2(BT)=\Hom(T,S^1)\otimes\Q$. For a polynomial in $q$ we write $q$ for the class of degree~$2$, so $P_X(q)=\sum_j\beta_{2j}(X)q^j$ when $H^{\mathrm{odd}}(X)=0$.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Weights, face posets and GKM manifolds}
Let $p\in X^T$. The tangent representation decomposes as $T_pX\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^nV(\alpha_{p,j})$, where $V(\alpha)=\C$ with $t\cdot z=\alpha(t)z$. Without an invariant complex structure the weights $\alpha_{p,j}\in\Hom(T,S^1)$ are defined up to sign, which does not affect linear dependence. The point $p$ is isolated if and only if no weight vanishes. For an oriented $T$-vector bundle, the equivariant Euler class of $T_pX$ at a fixed point is $e_T(T_pX)=\pm\prod_j\alpha_{p,j}\in H^{2n}(BT)$.

Let $X_j\subset X$ be the union of the orbits of dimension at most $j$. A \emph{face} of rank $j$ is the closure of a connected component of $X_j/T\setminus X_{j-1}/T$ that contains a fixed point, and $S(X)$ is the set of faces ordered by inclusion.

\begin{theorem}[Ayzenberg--Cherepanov~{\cite[Thm.~1]{AC22}}]\label{thm:AC}
For every $p\in X^T$, the poset $S(X)_{\geqslant p}$ of faces containing $p$ is isomorphic to the lattice of flats of the linear matroid of the weights $\alpha_{p,1},\dots,\alpha_{p,n}\in\Hom(T,S^1)\otimes\Q$. In particular $S(X)$ is graded, and each $S(X)_{\geqslant p}$ is a geometric lattice.
\end{theorem}

The action is \emph{equivariantly formal} if the Serre spectral sequence of $X_T\to BT$ degenerates; for $X^T$ finite and nonempty this holds if and only if $H^{\mathrm{odd}}(X)=0$, and then $\HT(X)\cong H^*(X)\otimes H^*(BT)$ as graded $H^*(BT)$-modules. A \emph{GKM manifold} is an equivariantly formal $X$ whose weights at each fixed point are pairwise linearly independent. Its fixed points and invariant $2$-spheres form the \emph{GKM graph}, whose edge $pq$ is labelled by the weight $\alpha_{pq}$ along the sphere, and by the GKM theorem~\cite{GKM98,CS74}
\[
\HT(X)\cong\bigl\{(f_p)\in\textstyle\bigoplus_{p\in X^T}H^*(BT)\bigm| f_p-f_q\in(\alpha_{pq})\text{ for every edge }pq\bigr\}.
\]

\subsection{Hessenberg varieties}
A \emph{Hessenberg function} is a non-decreasing map $h\colon[n]\to[n]$ with $h(j)\geqslant j$. For $M=\diag(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$ with pairwise distinct real $\lambda_i$, the \emph{regular semisimple Hessenberg variety} is
\[
\Hess(M,h)=\{V_\bullet\in\Fl(\C^n)\mid MV_i\subset V_{h(i)}\text{ for all }i\}.
\]
It is a smooth projective variety of complex dimension $\sum_j(h(j)-j)$ with $H^{\mathrm{odd}}=0$~\cite{DPS92,AH20}, invariant under the diagonal torus $T=T^n$. The fixed points are the $n!$ coordinate flags, indexed by $w\in S_n$, and the tangent weights at $w$ are $t_{w(i)}-t_{w(j)}$ for $j<i\leqslant h(j)$~\cite{DPS92,Tym08}. Let $\Gamma_h$ be the graph on $[n]$ with edges $\{j,i\}$, $j<i\leqslant h(j)$. Up to isomorphism, the graphs $\Gamma_h$ are exactly the indifference (unit interval) graphs~\cite{AB21}. By~\cite{DPS92},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dps}
P_{\Hess(M,h)}(q)=\sum_{w\in S_n}q^{\ell_h(w)},\qquad \ell_h(w)=\#\{(j,i)\mid j<i\leqslant h(j),\ w(j)>w(i)\}.
\end{equation}

Let $\bar k=(k_1,\dots,k_s)$ be a composition of $n$, $K_b=k_1+\dots+k_b$, and $h$ a Hessenberg function on $[s]$. The \emph{partial Hessenberg variety} $\Hess_{\bar k}(M,h)$ is the set of partial flags $V_{K_1}\subset\dots\subset V_{K_s}=\C^n$ with $MV_{K_a}\subset V_{K_{h(a)}}$ for all~$a$. It is smooth (Lemma~\ref{lem:fibration}). Its fixed points are the ordered set partitions $J=(J_1,\dots,J_s)$ of $[n]$ with $|J_a|=k_a$, and its tangent weights at $J$ are $t_y-t_x$ for $x\in J_a$, $y\in J_b$, $a<b\leqslant h(a)$~\cite[§5]{AB24}. In~\cite{AB24} this variety is denoted $M'_{\bar k,\Gamma_h,\lambda}$ and is defined by the conditions $ML_a\subset\bigoplus_bL_b$, the sum over $b=a$ and the neighbours $b$ of $a$ in $\Gamma_h$, on the orthogonal pieces $L_a=V_{K_a}\cap V_{K_{a-1}}^\perp$. Since $M$ is real and diagonal, hence Hermitian, $ML_a\perp L_b$ if and only if $ML_b\perp L_a$, and the two definitions agree. For a generic one-parameter subgroup, the Białynicki-Birula decomposition gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bb}
P_{\Hess_{\bar k}(M,h)}(q)=\sum_Jq^{\operatorname{ind}(J)},\qquad\operatorname{ind}(J)=\#\{(x,y)\mid x\in J_a,\ y\in J_b,\ a<b\leqslant h(a),\ x>y\},
\end{equation}
which reduces to~\eqref{eq:dps} for $\bar k=(1,\dots,1)$. The \emph{inflation} $\Gamma_h(\bar k)$ is the graph obtained from $\Gamma_h$ by replacing each vertex $a$ by $k_a$ pairwise non-adjacent copies, copies of $a$ and $b$ being adjacent iff $ab$ is an edge of $\Gamma_h$. At the fixed point $J$, identifying $x\in J_a$ with a copy of~$a$, the tangent weights are $t_x-t_y$ over the edges $xy$ of $\Gamma_h(\bar k)$, so the local weight matroid is the graphic matroid $M(\Gamma_h(\bar k))$.

\subsection{Isospectral manifolds and cluster-permutohedra}
Let $\Gamma$ be a connected graph on $[n]$ and $\lambda$ a generic spectrum. The \emph{isospectral manifold} $M_{\Gamma,\lambda}$ is the space of Hermitian matrices $A$ with spectrum $\lambda$ and $a_{ij}=0$ whenever $\{i,j\}\notin E(\Gamma)$, with $T^n$ acting by conjugation~\cite{AB24}. For $\Gamma=K_n$ it is $\Fl(\C^n)$.

\begin{definition}[{\cite[Def.~2.14]{AB24}}]\label{def:cluster}
Let $\Gamma$ be a simple graph on $[n]$. A \emph{clustering} is a partition $C=\{V_1,\dots,V_m\}$ of $[n]$ into blocks inducing connected subgraphs; ordered by refinement, the clusterings form the lattice of flats $\Flats(\Gamma)$ of the graphic matroid. An \emph{assignment} for $C$ is a family of label sets $L_1,\dots,L_m$ partitioning $[n]$ with $|L_i|=|V_i|$. The \emph{cluster-permutohedron} $\Cl_\Gamma$ is the set of pairs (clustering, assignment), where $(C',A')\leqslant(C,A)$ if $C'$ refines $C$ and the label set of each block of $C$ is the union of the label sets of the blocks of $C'$ it contains. It is graded by $\rk(C,A)=n-|C|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Ayzenberg--Buchstaber~{\cite[Thm.~1, Cor.~2.43]{AB24}}, \cite{AB21}]\label{thm:AB}
For connected $\Gamma$ and generic $\lambda$, $S(M_{\Gamma,\lambda})\cong\Cl_\Gamma$. If $\Gamma_h$ is connected, then $\Hess(M,h)$ and $M_{\Gamma_h,\lambda}$ are twins: they have homeomorphic orbit spaces by a homeomorphism preserving orbit dimensions, equivariant cohomology rings that are isomorphic as graded rings, and equal Betti numbers~\cite[Thm.~3.11]{AB21}. In particular $S(\Hess(M,h))\cong\Cl_{\Gamma_h}$. The isomorphism of equivariant cohomology is not one of $H^*(BT)$-algebras in general: the weights of $M_{\Gamma_h,\lambda}$ at the fixed point $w$ are $t_i-t_j$, those of $\Hess(M,h)$ are $t_{w(i)}-t_{w(j)}$. The face poset of a partial Hessenberg variety is described in Lemma~\ref{lem:partial-faces}, following~\cite[Thm.~2]{AB24}.
\end{theorem}

\section{Local equivariant cohomology}\label{sec:local}
%======================================================================

Let $V\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^nV(\alpha_j)$ be a representation of $T$ with all $\alpha_j\neq0$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sphere}
Restriction to $0$ is an injective map $\HT(V,V\setminus0)\to\HT(0)=H^*(BT)$ with image the principal ideal $(\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_n)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose an invariant complex structure on each $V(\alpha_j)$, which orients $V$. The group $\HT(V,V\setminus0)$ is the cohomology of the Thom space of the oriented bundle $V_T=ET\times_TV\to BT$. By the Thom isomorphism~\cite{MSt74} it is free of rank one over $H^*(BT)$ on the Thom class $\tau$, and $\tau$ restricts to the Euler class $e(V_T)=\prod_j\alpha_j$ in $\HT(V)=\HT(0)$. Restriction to $0$ is therefore multiplication by $\prod_j\alpha_j$, which is injective since $H^*(BT)$ is a domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ufd}
Let $k$ be a field and $R=k[t_1,\dots,t_m]$.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\arabic*)]
\item Every nonzero linear form is irreducible, hence prime, in $R$.
\item If $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$ and $\beta_1,\dots,\beta_{n'}$ are nonzero linear forms with $(\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_n)=(\beta_1\cdots\beta_{n'})$, then $n=n'$ and there are $\sigma\in S_n$ and $c_j\in k^\times$ with $\beta_{\sigma(j)}=c_j\alpha_j$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) A factorization of a linear form has a factor of degree $0$, a unit. (2) Equal principal ideals of a domain have associate generators, and both products are factorizations into primes by~(1); uniqueness of factorization in $R$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $pt\in X^T$ be an isolated fixed point with tangent weights $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\arabic*)]
\item The composition $\HT(X,X\setminus pt)\to\HT(X)\to\HT(pt)$ is injective, with image $(\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_n)=(e_T(T_{pt}X))$.
\item This ideal determines the multiset $\{\alpha_j\}$ up to nonzero scalars. Hence it determines the linear matroid of weights at $pt$, including parallel elements, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:AC} the lattice $S(X)_{\geqslant pt}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the slice theorem~\cite{Bre72}, $pt$ has an invariant closed neighbourhood $D$ equivariantly diffeomorphic to the unit disc of $V=T_{pt}X$. Excision of $X\setminus\operatorname{int}D$ and radial deformation give $\HT(X,X\setminus pt)\cong\HT(D,D\setminus pt)\cong\HT(V,V\setminus0)$, compatibly with restriction to $pt$. Part~(1) follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:sphere}, and part~(2) from Lemma~\ref{lem:ufd}, since rescaling does not change linear dependence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
As an abstract module, $\HT(X,X\setminus pt)$ is free of rank one and carries no information; everything lies in its embedding into $\HT(pt)$. For a GKM manifold the image can also be computed from the GKM theorem, as $\bigcap_j(\alpha_j)$. Without pairwise independence this intersection differs from the product: for $\alpha_1=\alpha_2$ it is $(\alpha_1)$, not $(\alpha_1^2)$. The Thom isomorphism argument needs no such assumption, which is why Theorem~\ref{thm:main} sees multiplicities.
\end{remark}

\section{Local weight matroids do not determine equivariant cohomology}\label{sec:counterexample}
%======================================================================

Let $M=\diag(1,2,3,4,5)$, $T=T^5$, and
\[
h_1=(2,3,5,5,5),\quad h_2=(2,4,4,5,5),\quad X_1=\Hess(M,h_1),\quad X_2=\Hess(M,h_2).
\]
So $X_1=\{MV_1\subset V_2,\ MV_2\subset V_3\}$ and $X_2=\{MV_1\subset V_2,\ MV_2\subset V_4,\ MV_3\subset V_4\}$. The graph $\Gamma_{h_1}$ has edges $12,23,34,35,45$: a triangle $\{3,4,5\}$ with the path $1-2-3$ attached. The graph $\Gamma_{h_2}$ has edges $12,23,24,34,45$: a triangle $\{2,3,4\}$ with two pendant edges. They are not isomorphic.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local-hess}
At every fixed point $w$ of $\Hess(M,h)$, the weight matroid is isomorphic to the graphic matroid $M(\Gamma_h)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The weights at $w$ are $e_{w(i)}-e_{w(j)}$ over the edges $\{j,i\}$ of $\Gamma_h$. The linear automorphism $e_a\mapsto e_{w^{-1}(a)}$ carries them to $e_i-e_j$, which realize $M(\Gamma_h)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:iso-matroids}
$M(\Gamma_{h_1})\cong M(\Gamma_{h_2})\cong U_{2,3}\oplus U_{1,1}\oplus U_{1,1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In each graph the only cycle is the triangle and the other two edges are bridges.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:counterexample}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\arabic*)]
\item $X_1$ and $X_2$ are connected smooth projective varieties of complex dimension $5$ with $H^{\mathrm{odd}}=0$. The torus $T$ acts on both with the same $120$ fixed points, as GKM actions whose effective quotient $T/\Delta(S^1)\cong T^4$ acts with complexity one.
\item For all $p_1\in X_1^T$ and $p_2\in X_2^T$ the weight matroids are isomorphic to $U_{2,3}\oplus U_{1,1}\oplus U_{1,1}$. Hence $S(X_1)_{\geqslant p_1}\cong S(X_2)_{\geqslant p_2}$, and the Chow rings, augmented Chow rings, intersection cohomology modules, Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomials and Orlik--Solomon algebras of the local matroids are isomorphic. Their Hilbert functions are $(1,14,14,1)$ for the Chow ring, $(1,19,44,19,1)$ for the augmented Chow ring, $(1,5,8,5,1)$ for the intersection cohomology module and $(1,5,9,7,2)$ for the Orlik--Solomon algebra, and the Kazhdan--Lusztig polynomial is~$1$.
\item $P_{X_1}(q)=1+17q+42q^2+42q^3+17q^4+q^5$ and $P_{X_2}(q)=1+12q+47q^2+47q^3+12q^4+q^5$. In particular $\HT(X_1)\not\cong\HT(X_2)$, even as graded vector spaces.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(3) Summing~\eqref{eq:dps} over $S_5$ gives the polynomials. Independently, the GKM presentation of $\HT(X_i)$, computed in Macaulay2 as a graded $\Q[t_1,\dots,t_5]$-module, has Hilbert series $P_{X_i}(q)/(1-q)^5$ with the same numerators. Since the actions are equivariantly formal, $\dim H^2_T(X_i)=\beta_2+5\beta_0$, which is $22$ for $X_1$ and $17$ for $X_2$.

(1) Smoothness, dimension and $H^{\mathrm{odd}}=0$ were recalled in Section~\ref{sec:prelim}; connectedness follows from $\beta_0=1$. The weights at a fixed point are distinct roots $e_a-e_b$, hence pairwise independent. An element of $T$ acting trivially lies in the common kernel of the weights $e_a-e_b$ over the edges of a connected graph, which is the diagonal circle, and the diagonal circle acts trivially on $\Fl(\C^5)$. So the effective torus has dimension $4$ and the complexity is $5-4=1$.

(2) follows from Lemmas~\ref{lem:local-hess} and~\ref{lem:iso-matroids} and Theorem~\ref{thm:AC}.
\end{proof}

\section{The face poset determines the Betti numbers in type A}\label{sec:rigidity}

\begin{question}[\cite{Mik22}]\label{q:betti}
Let $X_1,X_2$ carry torus actions with $H^{\mathrm{odd}}(X_1)=H^{\mathrm{odd}}(X_2)=0$. Does $S(X_1)\cong S(X_2)$ imply $\beta_j(X_1)=\beta_j(X_2)$ for all $j$?
\end{question}

For quasitoric manifolds the answer is yes, since $\beta_{2i}=h_i$ of the orbit polytope. The converse fails already there: a pentagonal prism and a cube with one vertex cut off have the same $h$-vector but different face lattices.

\subsection{Rigidity of cluster-permutohedra}
For $y\in\Cl_\Gamma$ let $\nu(y)$ be the number of minimal elements below~$y$. An element $y=(C,A)$ lies above the minimal element given by a bijection $p\colon[n]\to[n]$ exactly when $p$ maps every block onto its label set, so $\nu(y)=\prod_{V_i\in C}|V_i|!$. In particular $\Cl_\Gamma$ has $n!$ minimal elements.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:atoms-nu}
Let $x\in\Cl_\Gamma$ be minimal. Then $(\Cl_\Gamma)_{\geqslant x}\cong\Flats(\Gamma)$, and its atoms correspond to the edges of~$\Gamma$. The join of the atoms of a set $S$ of edges has rank $n-c(S)$, where $c(S)$ is the number of components of $([n],S)$. For distinct edges $e,f$, the join of their atoms has $\nu=6$ if $e$ and $f$ share a vertex and $\nu=4$ otherwise. Three pairwise adjacent edges either form a triangle, and then the join of their atoms has rank~$2$, or share a vertex, and then it has rank~$3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Above $x$ the assignment is determined by the clustering, and the join of the atoms of $S$ is the clustering into the components of $([n],S)$. For two edges this clustering has one block of size $3$, or two blocks of size $2$. Three pairwise adjacent edges span $3$ vertices if they form a triangle and $4$ otherwise.
\end{proof}

Call a set $F$ of edges of a simple graph a \emph{star family} if any two edges of $F$ share a vertex and no three form a triangle. Let $E(v)$ be the set of edges at a vertex $v$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:stars}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\arabic*)]
\item All edges of a star family with at least two edges contain a common vertex.
\item The maximal star families with at least two edges are exactly the sets $E(v)$ with $\deg v\geqslant2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Let $uv$ and $vw$ be in the family. A third edge meets both; if it avoids $v$, it is $uw$, and the three edges form a triangle. (2) If $\deg v\geqslant2$, $E(v)$ is a star family. An edge $g\notin E(v)$ meeting every edge of $E(v)$ meets each at its other endpoint; as these are distinct, $E(v)=\{vu,vw\}$ and $g=uw$, a triangle. So $E(v)$ is maximal, and by~(1) every maximal star family with at least two edges is of this form.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:edge-iso}
Let $\varphi\colon E(\Gamma)\to E(\Gamma')$ be a bijection between the edge sets of simple graphs such that $\varphi$ and $\varphi^{-1}$ preserve adjacency of edges and send triangles to triangles. Then $\varphi$ is induced by a bijection $\psi$ between the non-isolated vertices: $\varphi(uv)=\psi(u)\psi(v)$ for every edge $uv$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:stars}, $\varphi(E(v))=E(\psi(v))$ defines a bijection $\psi$ between the vertices of degree at least~$2$. Let $uv$ be an edge. If $\deg u,\deg v\geqslant2$, then $\varphi(uv)\in E(\psi(u))\cap E(\psi(v))$, so $\varphi(uv)=\psi(u)\psi(v)$. If $\deg u\geqslant2$ and $\deg v=1$, then $\varphi(uv)=\psi(u)v'$; were $\deg v'\geqslant2$, we would have $v'=\psi(w)$ with $uv\in E(w)$, so $w=v$, which is impossible. Put $\psi(v)=v'$, which is well defined since $v$ lies on one edge only. If $\deg u=\deg v=1$, then $uv$ is a component $K_2$, and by the previous cases applied to $\varphi^{-1}$ so is $\varphi(uv)$; map $u,v$ to its endpoints. The same construction for $\varphi^{-1}$ gives the inverse of~$\psi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:cl-rigid}
Let $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma'$ be simple graphs on $[n]$ and $[n']$, $n,n'\geqslant1$. If $\Cl_\Gamma\cong\Cl_{\Gamma'}$, then $\Gamma\cong\Gamma'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi$ be an isomorphism. Counting minimal elements gives $n!=n'!$, so $n=n'$. Fix a minimal $x$. Then $\Phi$ restricts to an isomorphism of lattices $(\Cl_\Gamma)_{\geqslant x}\to(\Cl_{\Gamma'})_{\geqslant\Phi(x)}$ and induces a bijection $\varphi\colon E(\Gamma)\to E(\Gamma')$ on atoms. Since $\Phi$ preserves joins in these intervals, rank and $\nu$, Lemma~\ref{lem:atoms-nu} shows that $\varphi^{\pm1}$ preserve adjacency of edges and triangles. By Proposition~\ref{prop:edge-iso}, $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma'$ are isomorphic after removing isolated vertices, and both have $n$ vertices.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Adjacency alone is the line graph. Whitney's theorem~\cite{Whi32} together with the rank of three edges, which separates $K_3$ from $K_{1,3}$, would also prove Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-rigid}. For the partial case, where a partition of the vertices must be tracked as well, one would need the edge isomorphism to be induced by a vertex bijection; by Jung's theorem~\cite{Jun66} this holds for connected graphs with more than four vertices but fails for $K_{1,3}+e$, $K_4-e$ and $K_4$. Proposition~\ref{prop:edge-iso} uses the triangles that the poset sees, and has no exceptions.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:q1-hess}
Let each of $X$ and $X'$ be either $M_{\Gamma,\lambda}$ with $\Gamma$ connected and $\lambda$ generic, or $\Hess(M,h)$ with $\Gamma_h$ connected. If $S(X)\cong S(X')$, then $\beta_j(X)=\beta_j(X')$ for all $j$, and $\HT(X)\cong\HT(X')$ as graded rings. If $X$ and $X'$ are both Hessenberg varieties, this is an isomorphism of $H^*(BT)$-algebras; if both are isospectral manifolds, it is one up to an automorphism of~$T$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:AB} the face posets are $\Cl_\Gamma$ and $\Cl_{\Gamma'}$ for the corresponding graphs, so $\Gamma\cong\Gamma'$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-rigid}. If $\pi$ is a permutation with $\pi(\Gamma)=\Gamma'$ and $P_\pi$ its permutation matrix, conjugation $A\mapsto P_\pi AP_\pi^{-1}$ preserves the spectrum and the zero pattern up to relabelling, $(P_\pi AP_\pi^{-1})_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}=a_{ij}$. It is therefore a diffeomorphism $M_{\Gamma,\lambda}\to M_{\Gamma',\lambda}$, equivariant along the automorphism $t\mapsto P_\pi tP_\pi^{-1}$ of $T$. This proves the corollary for two isospectral manifolds. A Hessenberg variety and its twin $M_{\Gamma_h,\lambda}$ have equal Betti numbers and equivariant cohomology rings isomorphic as graded rings (Theorem~\ref{thm:AB}), which gives the general case. Finally, let $X=\Hess(M,h)$ and $X'=\Hess(M,h')$ with $\pi(\Gamma_h)=\Gamma_{h'}$. In the GKM description, $\HT(X)$ consists of the $(f_w)_{w\in S_n}$ with $f_w-f_{w\circ(i\,j)}\in(t_{w(i)}-t_{w(j)})$ for every edge $ij$ of $\Gamma_h$. Since $(\pi(i)\,\pi(j))\circ\pi=\pi\circ(i\,j)$, the map $(f_w)\mapsto(f'_{w'})$, $f'_{w'}=f_{w'\pi}$, sends these conditions to the conditions for $\Gamma_{h'}$, and it is $H^*(BT)$-linear.
\end{proof}

The corollary does not use $H^{\mathrm{odd}}=0$. In particular it covers $M_{K_{1,3},\lambda}$ and the manifolds $M_{C_n,\lambda}$, $n\geqslant4$, which are not equivariantly formal~\cite{AB24,AMS23}.

\subsection{Partial Hessenberg varieties}\label{sec:partial}
Let $X=\Hess_{\bar k}(M,h)$ and $G=\Gamma_h(\bar k)$. For a partition $W=\{W_1,\dots,W_l\}$ of $[n]$ let $T_W\subset T$ be the subtorus of the elements whose coordinates are equal within every block. The following description of the faces is~\cite[Thm.~2]{AB24}, in the form in which it appears in the proof given there. As Theorem~\ref{thm:q1-partial} depends on its details, we include a proof.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:partial-faces}
The faces of $X$ correspond to the families $\{(W_j,c_j)\}_{j=1}^l$, where $W_1,\dots,W_l$ are the blocks of a partition of $[n]$, each $c_j\in\Z_{\geqslant0}^s$ is a vector of part counts with $\sum_ac_j(a)=|W_j|$, $\sum_jc_j=\bar k$, and for every $j$ either $|W_j|=1$, or the support $\{a\mid c_j(a)>0\}$ has at least two elements and induces a connected subgraph of~$\Gamma_h$. The fixed points of the face are the $J$ with $|J_a\cap W_j|=c_j(a)$ for all $a$ and $j$, so their number is
\[
\nu\bigl(\{(W_j,c_j)\}\bigr)=\prod_j\frac{|W_j|!}{\prod_ac_j(a)!}.
\]
The rank of the face is $n-l$, and $\{(W'_i,c'_i)\}\leqslant\{(W_j,c_j)\}$ if and only if every $W'_i$ lies in some $W_j$ and $c_j=\sum_{W'_i\subset W_j}c'_i$ for all~$j$. For every fixed point $J$, $S(X)_{\geqslant J}\cong\Flats(G)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A subgroup $H\subset T$ has the same invariant subspaces in $\C^n$ as $T_W$, where $W$ is the partition of $[n]$ into the classes on which the characters $t_x|_H$ coincide. Hence every stabilizer is some $T_W$, and the faces are the connected components $Y$ of the sets $X^{T_W}$ for which $T_W$ is the stabilizer of a generic point of~$Y$; such a face has rank $n-l$.

A $T_W$-invariant subspace is the direct sum of its intersections with the $\C^{W_j}$, so a flag in $X^{T_W}$ is a direct sum of flags $V^{(j)}_\bullet$ in $\C^{W_j}$. Since $M$ preserves $\C^{W_j}$, the sum satisfies the Hessenberg condition if and only if every summand does. The dimensions $c_j(a)=\dim V^{(j)}_{K_a}-\dim V^{(j)}_{K_{a-1}}$ are locally constant, and the flags with given $c$ form the set
\[
Y(W,c)\cong\prod_{j=1}^l\Hess_{c_j}(M|_{\C^{W_j}},h),
\]
where the parts $a$ with $c_j(a)=0$ are omitted, so that the $j$th factor is the partial Hessenberg variety of the induced subgraph of $\Gamma_h$ on the support of~$c_j$. Its fixed points are as stated. At a fixed point $J$, the tangent weights of $Y(W,c)$ are the weights $t_y-t_x$ of $X$ with $x$ and $y$ in one block. They span a space of dimension $n-l$ if and only if every block induces a connected subgraph of $G$, the vertices of $G$ being labelled through $J$; that is, if and only if every block is a single element or has counts supported on a connected set of at least two parts. If this holds, every factor is connected, by~\eqref{eq:bb} or by Lemma~\ref{lem:fibration}, so $Y(W,c)$ is connected with generic stabilizer $T_W$ and is a face. If it fails, the generic stabilizer of each component of $Y(W,c)$ is larger than $T_W$, and the component is counted for a finer partition.

Two faces $Y'\subset Y$ have generic stabilizers $T_{W'}\supset T_W$, so $W'$ refines $W$, and a common fixed point shows that the counts add up; the converse holds because $Y'$ is connected, is fixed by $T_W$ and meets $Y$. The faces containing $J$ correspond to the partitions $W$ whose blocks induce connected subgraphs of $G$ labelled through $J$, which are the flats of the graphic matroid of~$G$, in accordance with Theorem~\ref{thm:AC}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:q1-partial}
If $X$ and $X'$ are partial regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties with $S(X)\cong S(X')$, then $\beta_j(X)=\beta_j(X')$ for all~$j$.
\end{theorem}

The proof rests on three lemmas.

\begin{lemma}[Splitting]\label{lem:split}
Let $Q_1,\dots,Q_r$ be the connected components of $\Gamma_h$ and $P_{Q_i}$ the Poincaré polynomial of the partial Hessenberg variety of the restriction of $(\bar k,h)$ to~$Q_i$. Then
\[
P_X(q)=\#X^T\cdot\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{P_{Q_i}(q)}{P_{Q_i}(1)},
\]
and a component consisting of a single part contributes the factor~$1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The components of the indifference graph $\Gamma_h$ are intervals of parts, and every pair counted in~\eqref{eq:bb} lies in one component. Put $U_i=\bigcup_{a\in Q_i}J_a$. The pairs counted inside $Q_i$ depend only on the relative order of the elements of $U_i$, that is, on a fixed point of the variety of $Q_i$. Summing over the choices of $U_1,\dots,U_r$ gives $P_X=\binom{n}{|U_1|,\dots,|U_r|}\prod_iP_{Q_i}$, and setting $q=1$ gives $\#X^T=\binom{n}{|U_1|,\dots,|U_r|}\prod_iP_{Q_i}(1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fibration]\label{lem:fibration}
Put $\tilde h(i)=K_{h(a)}$ for $K_{a-1}<i\leqslant K_a$, and let $\pi\colon\Fl(\C^n)\to\Fl_{\bar k}$ be the projection. Then $\pi^{-1}(X)=\Hess(M,\tilde h)$, the restriction of $\pi$ is a fibre bundle with fibre $\prod_a\Fl(\C^{k_a})$, and
\[
P_{\Hess(M,\tilde h)}(q)=P_X(q)\cdot\prod_{a=1}^s[k_a]_q!.
\]
Moreover, $\Gamma_{\tilde h}$ is the inflation $\Gamma_h(\bar k)$ with every part made a clique.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A full flag $F_\bullet$ lies in $\Hess(M,\tilde h)$ iff $MF_i\subset F_{\tilde h(i)}$ for all~$i$. For $i$ in part $a$ we have $F_i\subset F_{K_a}$ and $\tilde h(i)=\tilde h(K_a)$, so the condition at $i$ follows from the condition at $K_a$, and the conditions at $K_1,\dots,K_s$ say that $\pi(F_\bullet)\in X$. The projection $\pi$ is a holomorphic bundle with fibre $\prod_a\Fl(V_{K_a}/V_{K_{a-1}})$ over $V_\bullet$, so it restricts to a bundle over~$X$. In particular $X$ is smooth, since $\Hess(M,\tilde h)$ is smooth and $\pi$ is a submersion. The cohomology of the fibre is generated by Chern classes of tautological bundles defined on the total space, and all spaces involved have only even cohomology, so the Leray--Hirsch theorem gives the product formula, with $P_{\Fl(\C^k)}=[k]_q!$. Finally, $i<j$ is an edge of $\Gamma_{\tilde h}$ iff $j\leqslant K_{h(a)}$ for the part $a$ of $i$, that is, iff $j$ lies in part $a$ or in a part $b$ with $a<b\leqslant h(a)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Reconstruction]\label{lem:reconstruct}
Let $G=\Gamma_h(\bar k)$ with its partition into parts. The poset $S(X)$ determines $\#X^T$ and the multiset of isomorphism classes, as graphs with a vertex partition, of the components of $G$ that have an edge.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The number $\#X^T$ is the number of minimal elements. Fix a fixed point $J$ and identify $S(X)_{\geqslant J}$ with $\Flats(G)$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:partial-faces}. For edges $uv$ and $vw$ the join of their atoms has the single nontrivial block $\{u,v,w\}$, so by the formula for $\nu$ in that lemma $\nu=3$ if $u$ and $w$ lie in one part and $\nu=6$ otherwise, since adjacent vertices lie in different parts. Two disjoint edges give $\nu=4$. As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-rigid}, an isomorphism $\Phi\colon S(X)\to S(X')$ induces a bijection $\varphi$ between the edge sets of the inflations that preserves the rank and $\nu$ of the join of every set of edges. By the values of $\nu$, $\varphi^{\pm1}$ preserve adjacency, and since the rank statement of Lemma~\ref{lem:atoms-nu} holds verbatim for $\Flats(G)$, they preserve triangles. By Proposition~\ref{prop:edge-iso}, $\varphi$ is induced by a bijection $\psi$ between the non-isolated vertices.

The map $\psi$ preserves parts. Two distinct non-isolated vertices $u,w$ lie in one part iff they have a common neighbour $v$ with $\nu(a_{uv}\vee a_{vw})=3$: a non-isolated vertex is a copy of a part $a$ of a component $Q$ of $\Gamma_h$ with at least two parts, so the copies of $a$ have a common neighbour, and $\nu=3$ forces $u,w$ into one part. Since $\psi$ preserves adjacency and $\varphi$ preserves $\nu$, $\psi$ preserves this relation. Finally, the components of $G$ with an edge are the inflations $G_Q$ of the components $Q$ of $\Gamma_h$ with at least two parts, and copies of the remaining parts are isolated, so $\psi$ matches these components as graphs with parts.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:q1-partial}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:split} it suffices to show that $S(X)$ determines $\#X^T$ and $\prod_QP_Q(q)/P_Q(1)$ over the components $Q$ of $\Gamma_h$ with at least two parts. For such $Q$, Lemma~\ref{lem:fibration} gives $P_Q=P_{\Hess(M_Q,\tilde h_Q)}/\prod_{a\in Q}[k_a]_q!$. The graph $\Gamma_{\tilde h_Q}$ is connected and is obtained from $G_Q$ by making the parts cliques, and isomorphic connected graphs $\Gamma_{\tilde h}$ give Hessenberg varieties with equal Betti numbers (the twins are then equivariantly diffeomorphic, as in the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:q1-hess}). Hence $P_{\Hess(M_Q,\tilde h_Q)}$ depends only on the isomorphism class of $G_Q$ with its parts; so do the part sizes. Lemma~\ref{lem:reconstruct} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Remarks}
Theorem~\ref{thm:q1-partial} includes partial flag varieties ($\Gamma_h=K_s$) and Grassmannians, where it is classical, and the full case $\bar k=(1,\dots,1)$. For disconnected $\Gamma_h$ the variety $\Hess(M,h)$ is a disjoint union of products of smaller Hessenberg varieties, and Theorem~\ref{thm:q1-partial} covers it as well.

The statements of this subsection were rechecked by a program written independently of the proofs: Lemma~\ref{lem:partial-faces} against the faces computed from the GKM graph for all $186$ pairs $(\bar k,h)$ with $n\leqslant5$; formula~\eqref{eq:bb} against the number of $\mathbb F_q$-points of $\Hess_{\bar k}(M,h)$ for $n=4$ and $q=5,7$; and the statement that the data recovered in Lemma~\ref{lem:reconstruct} determine the Poincaré polynomial, for all pairs with $n\leqslant7$. Lemma~\ref{lem:fibration} and Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-rigid} were also checked by computer: the identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:fibration} for all $2\,949$ pairs $(\bar k,h)$ with $n\leqslant7$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:cl-rigid} by exact poset isomorphism for all $208$ graphs with $n\leqslant6$.

\section{Other Lie types}\label{sec:lie}
%======================================================================

Let $\Phi$ be a root system in a Euclidean space $V$, with Weyl group $W$, simple roots $\Delta$ and positive roots $\Phi^+$, and let $G\supset B\supset T$ be the corresponding complex reductive group, Borel subgroup and maximal torus. A \emph{Hessenberg ideal} is a subset $I\subset\Phi^+$ such that $\alpha\in I$, $\beta\in\Phi^+$ and $\alpha-\beta\in\Phi^+$ imply $\alpha-\beta\in I$. For a regular semisimple $s\in\operatorname{Lie}(T)$, the Hessenberg variety $\Hess(I)\subset G/B$ consists of the $gB$ with $\operatorname{Ad}(g^{-1})s\in\mathfrak b\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I}\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$. It is smooth and projective of dimension $|I|$ with $H^{\mathrm{odd}}=0$, its fixed points are the $w\in W$, the tangent weights at $w$ are $-w(\alpha)$, $\alpha\in I$, and the invariant curve of weight $-w(\alpha)$ joins $w$ to $ws_\alpha$~\cite{DPS92}. It is connected if and only if $\Delta\subset I$. Distinct positive roots are not proportional, so $\Hess(I)$ is a GKM manifold. For type $A_{n-1}$ and $I=\{e_j-e_i\mid j<i\leqslant h(j)\}$ it is $\Hess(M,h)$.

Let $M(I)$ be the linear matroid of $I\subset V$ and $\cL(I)$ its lattice of flats. For a flat $F$ let $W_F\subset W$ be the subgroup generated by the reflections $s_\alpha$, $\alpha\in F$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lie-faces}
The face poset of $X=\Hess(I)$ is the set of pairs $(F,wW_F)$ of a flat $F\in\cL(I)$ and a left coset of $W_F$, where $(F,wW_F)\leqslant(F',w'W_{F'})$ if and only if $F\subset F'$ and $wW_F\subset w'W_{F'}$. The rank of $(F,wW_F)$ is the rank of $F$, and the number of fixed points below it is $\nu(F,wW_F)=|W_F|$. In particular $S(X)_{\geqslant w}\cong\cL(I)$ for every~$w$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:AC} the faces through $w$ correspond to the flats $F$ of $M(I)$: the face $Y$ is the component through $w$ of $X^H$, where $H$ is the identity component of the common kernel of the characters $w(\alpha)$, $\alpha\in F$. As $Y$ is a smooth projective $T$-variety with isolated fixed points, any two of its fixed points are joined by a chain of invariant curves in~$Y$, and an invariant curve of $X$ through a fixed point of $Y$ lies in $Y$ if and only if its weight lies in the span of $w(F)$. Write a fixed point as $wu$. The curves at $wu$ have weights $-wu(\beta)$, $\beta\in I$, and lead to $wus_\beta$. For $u=1$ the admissible $\beta$ are those of $I\cap\operatorname{span}F=F$, so $u$ stays in $W_F$; and an element $u\in W_F$ preserves $\operatorname{span}F$, so $u(\beta)\in\operatorname{span}F$ is again equivalent to $\beta\in F$. Hence $Y^T=wW_F$. The tangent weights of $Y$ at $w$ recover $F$, so distinct pairs give distinct faces, and inclusion of faces is inclusion of tangent weights at a common fixed point.
\end{proof}

For type~A the flats are the clusterings of $\Gamma_h$, $W_F$ is the product of the symmetric groups of the blocks, and Proposition~\ref{prop:lie-faces} recovers $S(\Hess(M,h))\cong\Cl_{\Gamma_h}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lines}
Let $I\subset\Phi^+$ and $I'\subset\Phi'^+$ be Hessenberg ideals. Suppose that there is a linear isomorphism $g\colon V\to V'$ with $gWg^{-1}=W'$ that maps the set of lines $\{\R\alpha\mid\alpha\in I\}$ onto $\{\R\alpha'\mid\alpha'\in I'\}$. Then $\HT(\Hess(I))\cong H^*_{T'}(\Hess(I'))$ as graded rings, compatibly with the isomorphism $H^*(BT)\cong H^*(BT')$ induced by $g$, and the two varieties have equal Betti numbers.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The GKM graph of $\Hess(I)$ has vertex set $W$ and edges $\{w,ws_\alpha\}$, $\alpha\in I$, labelled by $\pm w(\alpha)$, so it depends only on $W$ and the lines of~$I$. The bijection $w\mapsto gwg^{-1}$ sends the edge $\{w,ws_\alpha\}$ to the edge $\{gwg^{-1},gwg^{-1}s_{g\alpha}\}$ and its label to $g(w(\alpha))=(gwg^{-1})(g\alpha)$, a multiple of the label of the image. The GKM theorem gives the isomorphism of rings, and the Betti numbers are determined by the Hilbert series of $\HT$ because both actions are equivariantly formal.
\end{proof}

Automorphisms of the Dynkin diagram and the duality $\alpha\leftrightarrow\alpha^\vee$, which identifies the lines of $B_n$ with those of $C_n$, are instances of Proposition~\ref{prop:lines}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cycles}
Let $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k$, $k\geqslant3$, be vectors with $(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=2$ such that $(\alpha_i,\alpha_{i+1})=\pm1$ for all $i$ modulo~$k$ and all other pairs are orthogonal, and let $\varepsilon=\prod_i(\alpha_i,\alpha_{i+1})$. Then the $\alpha_i$ span a space of dimension $k-1$ if $\varepsilon=(-1)^k$, and of dimension $k$ otherwise.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Gram matrix is $2+A$, where $A$ is a signed adjacency matrix of the $k$-cycle. Replacing some $\alpha_i$ by $-\alpha_i$ preserves $\varepsilon$, and we may assume that all entries of $A$ are $-1$ except possibly one, which is $-1$ exactly when $\varepsilon=(-1)^k$. In that case $2+A$ is the Cartan matrix of the affine diagram of type $A_{k-1}$, of corank one. Otherwise $-A$ is the adjacency matrix of a cycle with one negative edge, whose eigenvalues $2\cos((2j+1)\pi/k)$ are different from~$2$, and $2+A$ is nonsingular.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:simply-laced}
Let $\Phi$ and $\Phi'$ be simply-laced root systems and $I\supset\Delta$, $I'\supset\Delta'$ Hessenberg ideals. If there is an isomorphism of lattices $\cL(I)\to\cL(I')$ preserving $\nu(F)=|W_F|$ on the flats of rank~$2$, then there is an isometry $g\colon V\to V'$ with $gWg^{-1}=W'$ that maps the lines of $I$ onto the lines of~$I'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Normalize the roots to $(\alpha,\alpha)=2$, and let $\varphi\colon I\to I'$ be the induced bijection of atoms. If $\alpha,\beta\in I$ are orthogonal, the plane they span contains no further root and $\nu(\alpha\vee\beta)=4$. Otherwise the plane is of type $A_2$, and since $s_\alpha$ and $s_\beta$ generate its Weyl group, $\nu(\alpha\vee\beta)=6$. So $\varphi$ is an isomorphism of the graphs $N(I)$, $N(I')$ on $I$, $I'$ whose edges are the pairs of non-orthogonal roots.

Sign each edge $\alpha\beta$ of $N(I)$ by the sign of $(\alpha,\beta)$. For an induced cycle $C$ of $N(I)$ the rank of the join of its atoms in $\cL(I)$ is the dimension of the span of $C$, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:cycles} determines the product of the signs around~$C$. Since $\varphi$ preserves induced cycles and ranks of joins, the signings of $N(I)$ and $N(I')$ have the same product on every induced cycle. The induced cycles generate the cycle space of a graph, and the product of signs is a homomorphism on it; hence the products agree on all cycles, and two signings with this property differ by switching~\cite{Zas82}: there are $d_\alpha=\pm1$ with $(\varphi\alpha,\varphi\beta)=d_\alpha d_\beta(\alpha,\beta)$ for all $\alpha,\beta\in I$.

The families $(\alpha)_{\alpha\in I}$ and $(d_\alpha\varphi(\alpha))_{\alpha\in I}$ thus have the same Gram matrix. They span $V$ and $V'$, as they contain the simple roots, so there is an isometry $g$ with $g(\alpha)=d_\alpha\varphi(\alpha)$. It maps the lines of $I$ to the lines of $I'$ and satisfies $gs_\alpha g^{-1}=s_{\varphi(\alpha)}$. The group $W$ is generated by the reflections $s_\alpha$, $\alpha\in I$, because $\Delta\subset I$, and likewise $W'$; hence $gWg^{-1}=W'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:q1-ade}
Let $X=\Hess(I)$ and $X'=\Hess(I')$ be regular semisimple Hessenberg varieties of simply-laced types. If $S(X)\cong S(X')$, then $\beta_j(X)=\beta_j(X')$ for all~$j$. If $X$ and $X'$ are connected, then moreover $\HT(X)\cong H^*_{T'}(X')$ as graded rings.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
An isomorphism of face posets restricts to an isomorphism of the intervals above corresponding minimal elements, which are $\cL(I)$ and $\cL(I')$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:lie-faces}, and it preserves~$\nu$. In the connected case apply Theorem~\ref{thm:simply-laced} and Proposition~\ref{prop:lines}. In general let $J=\Delta\cap I$. Then $I$ lies in the positive roots of the subsystem $\Phi_J$ and contains its simple roots, $\Hess(I)$ is the disjoint union of $|W|/|W_J|$ copies of the Hessenberg variety of $(\Phi_J,I)$, and $W_J$ is generated by the $s_\alpha$, $\alpha\in I$. The argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:simply-laced} applies to $(\Phi_J,I)$ and $(\Phi'_{J'},I')$, so these varieties have equal Betti numbers and $|W_J|=|W'_{J'}|$, and the numbers of copies agree because $|W|$ is the number of minimal elements of~$S(X)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:non-simply-laced}
For Hessenberg varieties of type~A, Corollary~\ref{cor:q1-ade} gives a proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:q1-hess} that does not use twins. Propositions~\ref{prop:lie-faces} and~\ref{prop:lines} hold in every type. The first step of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:simply-laced} does not: in a plane of type $B_2$ or $G_2$ that contains at least three lines of $I$, $\nu$ is the order of the whole dihedral group and no longer tells which pairs of lines are orthogonal. We computed the lattice $\cL(I)$ with the function $\nu$, up to isomorphism, for all $1\,230$ Hessenberg ideals of the root systems $A_1$--$A_5$, $B_2$--$B_5$, $C_3$--$C_5$, $D_4$, $D_5$, $F_4$ and $G_2$. Two ideals with isomorphic data, of the same or of different types, always have equal Poincaré polynomials, and for connected varieties they always differ by a diagram automorphism or by duality. So the conclusion of Corollary~\ref{cor:q1-ade} holds for all types in these ranks.
\end{remark}

\section{A formula over the face poset}\label{sec:strata}
%======================================================================

In this section a torus $T$ acts on $X$ with isolated fixed points and with an invariant almost complex structure, so that the weights have signs, and $\xi$ is a one-parameter subgroup that is nonzero on all weights and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:morse}
P_Y(q)=\sum_{p\in Y^T}q^{\operatorname{ind}_Y(p)}\qquad\text{for $Y=X$ and for every face submanifold $Y$ of $X$,}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{ind}_Y(p)$ is the number of weights of $Y$ at $p$ that are negative on~$\xi$. This holds for smooth projective varieties with an algebraic torus action, by the Białynicki-Birula decomposition~\cite{BB73}, and for Hamiltonian actions, where $\langle\mu,\xi\rangle$ is a perfect Morse function. For a face $\sigma$ with face submanifold $X_\sigma$ and a fixed point $p\in X_\sigma$, let $N_p(\sigma)$ be the set of weights of $X_\sigma$ at $p$ that are negative on $\xi$, and $M(N_p(\sigma))$ its linear matroid. Recall that the Tutte polynomial of a matroid $M$ of rank $r$ on $E$ satisfies $T_M(1,y)=\sum_S(y-1)^{|S|-r}$, the sum over the spanning subsets $S\subset E$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:strata}
Under assumption~\eqref{eq:morse},
\[
P_X(q)=\sum_{\sigma\in S(X)}(q-1)^{\operatorname{rk}\sigma}e_\sigma(q),\qquad e_\sigma(q)=\sum_{p}T_{M(N_p(\sigma))}(1,q),
\]
where the second sum is over the fixed points $p$ of $X_\sigma$ such that $N_p(\sigma)$ spans the space of weights of $X_\sigma$ at~$p$. The same formula holds for every face submanifold, $P_{X_\tau}=\sum_{\sigma\leqslant\tau}(q-1)^{\operatorname{rk}\sigma}e_\sigma$, with the same $e_\sigma$; hence the $e_\sigma$ are determined by the Poincaré polynomials of the face submanifolds and do not depend on~$\xi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $N_p$ be the set of weights at $p$ negative on $\xi$. Then $q^{\operatorname{ind}(p)}=\sum_{S\subset N_p}(q-1)^{|S|}$. Sort the subsets $S$ by the flat $F$ that they span in the matroid of weights at~$p$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:AC} the pairs $(p,F)$ are the pairs of a face $\sigma$ and a fixed point $p\in X_\sigma$, the weights of $X_\sigma$ at $p$ being the elements of $F$; and $S$ runs over the subsets of $N_p(\sigma)=N_p\cap F$ that span~$F$. Therefore
\[
P_X(q)=\sum_\sigma(q-1)^{\operatorname{rk}\sigma}\sum_{p\in X_\sigma^T}\ \sum_{S\subset N_p(\sigma)\text{ spanning}}(q-1)^{|S|-\operatorname{rk}\sigma},
\]
and the inner sum is $T_{M(N_p(\sigma))}(1,q)$ if $N_p(\sigma)$ has rank $\operatorname{rk}\sigma$, and is empty otherwise. The faces, fixed points and weights of $X_\tau$ are those of $X$ contained in $X_\tau$, so the same computation gives the formula for~$X_\tau$. By induction on the rank these formulas determine the~$e_\sigma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
(1) If the weights of $X_\sigma$ at its fixed points are linearly independent, $N_p(\sigma)$ spans only when it consists of all the weights, that is, at the unique minimum of $\langle\mu,\xi\rangle$ on $X_\sigma$, and its matroid is free with $T=1$. So $e_\sigma=1$. For a toric manifold the theorem is the formula $h(q)=\sum_\sigma(q-1)^{\dim\sigma}$ for the $h$-polynomial of the orbit polytope.

(2) For $\Fl(\C^3)$ there are $6$ faces of rank $0$, $9$ of rank $1$, all with $e=1$, and the top face. There $N_w$ is the set of inversions of~$w$: the two permutations with two inversions contribute $1$ each and the longest one contributes $T_{U_{2,3}}(1,q)=q+2$. So $e=q+4$ and $P=6+9(q-1)+(q-1)^2(q+4)=1+2q+2q^2+q^3$.

(3) For a connected Hessenberg graph $\Gamma_h$ the top face has $e(q)=\sum_S(q-1)^{|S|-n+1}L(S)$, the sum over the connected spanning sets $S$ of edges, where $L(S)$ is the number of $w\in S_n$ with $w(i)>w(j)$ for all edges $\{i<j\}$ in~$S$. Without the weights $L(S)$ this would be $T_{\Gamma_h}(1,q)$, an invariant of the graphic matroid. The values are $1$ for a path, $q+4$ for $K_3$, $q^3+6q^2+20q+33$ for $K_4$, and $q+6$ for \emph{both} graphs of Theorem~\ref{thm:counterexample}. The two varieties of that theorem are thus distinguished only by their faces of lower rank.

(4) $e_\sigma$ is not a matroid invariant: $h=(2,3,5,6,6,6)$ and $h=(2,4,5,5,6,6)$ have isomorphic graphic matroids and $e=q^2+8q+28$, resp.\ $q^2+8q+33$.
\end{example}

The polynomial $e_\sigma$ has nonnegative coefficients, and $e_\sigma(1)$ is the total number of bases of the matroids $M(N_p(\sigma))$. Geometrically, when the complexified torus acts on a smooth projective $X$, the open stratum of $\sigma$ is a principal bundle over its quotient, which may be non-separated, with fibre a complex torus of dimension $\operatorname{rk}\sigma$, and $e_\sigma$ is the $E$-polynomial of the quotient.

Theorem~\ref{thm:strata} uses three kinds of data: the faces with their fixed points, the matroids of weights, and the signs of the weights on a common~$\xi$. The last is an oriented structure: at each fixed point, the oriented matroid of the weights together with the tope cut out by~$\xi$. The oriented matroid alone carries much less. For $\Hess(I)$ the signed weights at $w$ are the image under $w$ of those at the identity, so the oriented matroids at all fixed points are isomorphic; and in type~A they are graphic, hence regular, so that even their reorientation class is determined by the underlying matroid. At the other extreme, for a generic circle in $T$ the oriented matroid at $p$ is the pair $\{\operatorname{ind}(p),n-\operatorname{ind}(p)\}$, and these pairs determine the Betti numbers by Poincaré duality.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:moment}
Let a torus $H$ act on a compact symplectic manifold $X$ in a Hamiltonian fashion with isolated fixed points, with moment map $\mu$ and moment polytope $\Delta=\mu(X)$, and let the weights be taken for a compatible almost complex structure, with the sign for which $\mu$ increases along them. Then the weights at a fixed point $p$ lie in an open half-space if and only if $\mu(p)$ is a vertex of $\Delta$, and $\mu$ restricts to a bijection from these fixed points to the vertices of~$\Delta$. The weights at $p$ positively span $\mathfrak h^*$ if and only if $\mu(p)$ is an interior point of~$\Delta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $C_p$ be the cone spanned by the weights at~$p$. By the local normal form, $\mu$ maps a neighbourhood of $p$ onto a neighbourhood of the apex of $\mu(p)+C_p$. The fibres of $\mu$ are connected~\cite{Ati82,GS82}, and near a value $\mu(x)$ the polytope coincides with the local cone at any point $x$ of the fibre~\cite{Sja98}. So $\Delta$ coincides with $\mu(p)+C_p$ near $\mu(p)$. As the weights are nonzero, they lie in an open half-space exactly when $C_p$ is a pointed cone, that is, when $\mu(p)$ is a vertex, and they positively span $\mathfrak h^*$ exactly when $C_p=\mathfrak h^*$. The fibre over a vertex is connected and consists of fixed points, hence is a single point.
\end{proof}

For example, if a $2$-torus acts on $\C P^3$ through four characters, three of which are the vertices of a triangle containing the fourth, the weight matroid is $U_{2,3}$ at all four fixed points, and the oriented matroid distinguishes the interior fixed point from the other three.

\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}
%======================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Non-simply-laced types.} Extend Theorem~\ref{thm:simply-laced} to the types $B$, $C$, $F_4$, $G_2$: does the lattice $\cL(I)$ with the function $\nu$ determine the lines of $I$ and the group $W$ up to isometry? By Remark~\ref{rem:non-simply-laced} this holds in rank at most~$5$. The missing step is to recover the angles between the lines of $I$ inside planes of type $B_2$ and $G_2$. The same question may be asked for partial flag varieties $G/P$ and partial Hessenberg varieties outside type~A.
\item \emph{A formula without signs.} Theorem~\ref{thm:strata} computes $e_\sigma$ from the signs of the weights on a generic $\xi$, although the result does not depend on~$\xi$. Can $e_\sigma$ be computed from $S(X)$ alone? In complexity zero $e_\sigma=1$.
\item \emph{Beyond algebraic actions.} Question~\ref{q:betti} remains open for general equivariantly formal torus manifolds of positive complexity.
\item \emph{Subtori of rank two.} For a generic $2$-torus in $T$, the oriented matroid of the weights at a fixed point is a cyclic word. For all connected Hessenberg varieties $\Hess(M,h)$ with $n\leqslant6$ the multiset of these words over the fixed points determines the Betti numbers, and indeed the variety. Is there a reason, as there is for a circle?
\end{enumerate}
